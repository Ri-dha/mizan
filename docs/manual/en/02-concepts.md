## Buckets

Your monthly income is split into **buckets** by percentage. A bucket is not an account; it is a promise about how this month's money will be used. When income arrives it is allocated across the buckets, and every expense you log takes from one of them.

## The four figures

| Figure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Allocated** | This bucket's share of the income actually received this month. Before pay day you also see the planned share in smaller print. |
| **Committed** | Money already promised: unpaid bills due this month, this month's debt payment, and a goal deposit you planned but have not made. |
| **Spent** | Expenses logged against the bucket this month. |
| **Free** | Allocated minus committed minus spent, plus anything moved in, minus anything moved out. This is what you can still spend. |

Marking a bill paid moves its amount from **committed** to **spent**. Nothing is ever counted twice.

## Month start day

Salaries in Iraq often arrive on the 25th. In Settings you can make a month run from the 25th to the 24th so that a plan covers one pay cheque. The month is named after the calendar month it starts in: "September" with a start day of 25 runs from 25 September to 24 October.

## Transfers are not spending

Putting money into a goal, paying down a loan or buying gold moves wealth from one place to another. Mizan records these as **transfers**: they leave the bucket, but they do not count as spending and they do not reduce your net worth. Only consumption does.

## Whole dinars

Mizan stores every amount as whole dinars (or cents for dollars) and never rounds behind your back. When a percentage split leaves a dinar over, the largest bucket gets it, so the parts always add up to the whole.
